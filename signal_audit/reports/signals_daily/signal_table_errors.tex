\section{Summary}

\textbf{In one sentence.} In the signals table, the amounts and counts pass their checks (99 of 165 columns), but it has serious errors in time of play, recency and active days, and one of them uses data from the future. Several platform scores, including \code{churn_score}, are built on these columns.

\section{The 165 Columns}

The table has \textbf{165 columns}. Each one has its checks (\code{signal_checks.yaml}): its formula in the workbook, an accounting rule, a window that must be the sum of its daily values, a \code{prior_*} value that must be the one of the window before, or a limit (an impossible value, or a longer window smaller than a shorter one). A check \emph{passes} if it holds in at least 99\% of the rows of two snapshots (15 September and 1 October 2026). Several columns were also compared with the daily tables or with the transactions; this is how the errors of definition show up.

\begin{center}
\input{resumen_columnas_en}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Pass (99):} the ids, the windows of amounts and counts (stake, wins, GGR, deposits, withdrawals, cashout, bets, sessions), their \code{prior_*}, the accounting rules, and the scores that follow their formula on correct inputs. They can be used as they are.
  \item \textbf{Wrong (37)} and \textbf{affected (12):} points 1 to 22 of the list. A column can pass its checks and still be wrong: \code{active_days_*} is the sum of its daily value, but the daily value uses a wrong definition.
  \item \textbf{Unconfirmed (11):} the workbook does not give the full definition (points 23 to 25).
\end{itemize}
The list of the 165 columns, with their status, their point and their checks, is in annex~\ref{sec:columns}.

\textbf{Notation.} $D$: date of the row. $S$: stake in the window; $S_X$: stake of the bets that meet $X$. $W^{r}_d, W^{l}_d, W^{j}_d$: stake on day $d$ with real money, released bonus and playable bonus. $G$: GGR. $P$: amount won by the player. Session $s$: start $a_s$, end $b_s$, $n_s$ bets. \textbf{M053, M113, M127\ldots}: code of the measure in the data model workbook (\code{gld-schemas.xlsx}, sheet \emph{Measures}).

\section{Evaluation Criteria}\label{sec:criteria}
Every column is evaluated with the same criteria. This is the full list.

\textbf{1. The data.} Two snapshots of the table, 15 September and 1 October 2026; the five brands (14, 23, 64, 72 and 73); the latest version (\code{_version}) of each row; all the rows, no sampling. The windows and lags also use the days before each snapshot. The format, distribution and share checks use every day of September 2026.

\textbf{2. The checks of the table itself.} Each column has one or more in \code{signal_checks.yaml}. The table gives each kind, its rule and how many columns and checks use it; annex~\ref{sec:checks} gives the exact rule of each one.

\toprule
Kind & What it checks & Rule to pass & Columns & Checks\\
\midrule

\end{tabular}
\end{center}
$x$: the value of the column in the table. $f$: the expected value (the formula, the identity, the sum of the window or the value $k$ days before). The tolerance $0.01 + 0.001\,|f|$ lets the rounding of amounts pass: one cent plus 0.1\%. A column can have several checks (so there are 210 for 165 columns).

\textbf{3. The share that passes.} For each check, with the two snapshots together:
\[ \text{pass} = 100 \cdot \frac{\#\{\text{rows that meet the rule}\}}{\#\{\text{rows with a value}\}}. \]
Rows with NULL do not count. A check \emph{passes} if it holds in at least 99\% of the rows; it is \emph{almost} from 95\% to 99\%; and it \emph{fails} below 95\%.

\textbf{4. Comparisons with recalculated data.} A column can follow its own rules and still be wrongly defined: for example, \code{active_days_l30d} is the exact sum of its daily value, but the daily value uses another definition. So several columns are also compared with their recalculated value:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{From each bet of the transactions:} mean and median session length, gap between sessions, \code{play_secs}, night, weekend and hourly entropy.
  \item \textbf{From the daily gaming table:} \code{days_since_bet}, \code{active_days_*} and \code{tenure_days}.
\end{itemize}
The correct definition used in each case is in its point (field \emph{Definition}). What I recalculate from the transactions is compared as a distribution, not player by player: the \code{accountId} of the landing transactions is another identifier than the \code{player_id} of gold (2 of about 18,000 players match), for a reason I do not know; \code{partyId} is still to test.

\textbf{5. Impossible and suspicious values.} Each metric has a real-life range (\code{metrics.yaml}). \emph{Impossible}: a value that cannot come from real data, for example a share above 1 or a session of more than 1 day. \emph{Suspicious}: a value that is possible but very rare. They are counted per brand and day (section~\ref{sec:flags}).

\textbf{6. The status of each column.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \stWrong: it fails one of its checks, or the comparison of criterion 4 shows that it does not follow the workbook definition, even if it meets its rules.
  \item \stAffected: it follows its formula, but one of its inputs is wrong.
  \item \stUnconfirmed: the workbook does not give the full definition, so I cannot say if it is right.
  \item \stCorrect: none of the cases above, and all its checks pass (the worst holds in 99.92\% of the rows).
  \item \stUnchecked: technical keys and metadata (\code{_version}, \code{param_version}, \code{has_sufficient_data}), and the load times and the snapshot date (\code{_ingested_at}, \code{_processed_at}, \code{snapshot_date}), which cannot be checked with the lake files.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{7. The priority of each point.} \prioA{} it affects a platform score or a model today, in many rows. \prioM{} wrong in many rows but not used for decisions, or already fixed from now on. \prioB{} few rows, only the history, or a format detail. \prioQ{} the workbook does not give the definition.

\section{High Priority}
They affect a platform score or a model today, in many rows.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.},ref=\arabic*,leftmargin=*,series=issues]

\issue{it:recency}{\prioA\ \code{days_since_bet}}
{\pts{\item \textbf{Uses future data:} it matches the recency of 7 days later ($D+7$) for 92\% of the players.
\item \textbf{Effect:} AUC of 0.99 to predict a bet in the next 7 days, against 0.79--0.87 with the real recency.
\item \textbf{History:} 0 for almost every player until May 2026.}}
{\pts{\item \textbf{Table:} $R_D = (D+7) - \max\{d \le D+7 : \text{bet}\}$.
\item \textbf{Correct:} $R_D = D - \max\{d \le D : \text{bet}\}$.}}
{\pts{\item Calculate it with the daily activity up to $D$ (\code{days_since_last_bet} of the pipeline).}}
{\fig{fig:recency_zero}, \fig{fig:recency_shift}, \fig{fig:recency_proof}.}

\issue{it:activedays}{\prioA\ \code{active_days_today}, \code{active_days_l7d}, \code{active_days_l30d}, \code{active_days_l90d}, \code{active_days_l180d}, \code{prior_active_days_*}}
{\pts{\item \textbf{It counts days played only with playable bonus}, which are not active days.
\item \textbf{Evidence:} it matches a count of any wallet for 96\%--99.7\% of the players, and the official definition for only 48\%--86\%. It never counts fewer days.
\item \textbf{Effect:} 17\%--34\% of the players who bet in 30 days have 0 active days, and the table gives them at least one.
\item \textbf{Scope:} \code{active_days_today} too (the windows are its sum); \code{active_days_l180d} was not compared directly. Affects \code{frequency_decline} and \code{engagement_score}.}}
{\pts{\item \textbf{Table:} $A_w = \#\{d \in (D-w, D] : W^r_d + W^l_d + W^j_d > 0\}$.
\item \textbf{Correct (M053):} $A_w = \#\{d \in (D-w, D] : W^r_d + W^l_d > 0\}$.}}
{\pts{\item Count only the days with a stake from real money or released bonus.}}
{\fig{fig:active_match}, \fig{fig:false_actives}.}

\issue{it:churnscore}{\prioA\ \code{churn_score} (and \code{churn_propensity_raw}, \code{churn_band})}
{\pts{\item \textbf{It follows its formula, but with wrong inputs:} \code{days_since_bet} (point~\ref{it:recency}), the active days (point~\ref{it:activedays}) and \code{value_decline} with no upper limit (point~\ref{it:valuedecline}).
\item \textbf{In practice:} its AUC (0.78--0.83) does not show the leak.}}
{\pts{\item \textbf{Formula:} $\operatorname{clamp}_{[0,100]}(0.5\,R + 0.3\,F + 0.2\,M)$, with $R$ = \code{recency_pressure}, $F$ = \code{frequency_decline}, $M$ = \code{value_decline}.}}
{\pts{\item Recalculate it with the recency up to the date, the official active days and $M$ capped at $[0,100]$, or do not use it.}}
{\fig{fig:recency_proof}, \fig{fig:index_limits}.}

\issue{it:valuedecline}{\prioA\ \code{value_decline} (\code{monetary_score} in the workbook)}
{\pts{\item \textbf{No upper limit:} it reaches 57 million in September (802 million in the history).
\item \textbf{How much:} 0.84\% of the rows are above 100.
\item \textbf{Affects:} \code{churn_score}.}}
{\pts{\item \textbf{Workbook (M125) and table:} $M = 100 \max\bigl(0, 1 - \frac{G_7/7}{G_{90}/90}\bigr)$; no limit when $G_{90}$ is small or negative.
\item \textbf{Correct:} $\min(100, M)$.}}
{\pts{\item Cap it at $[0,100]$ and treat $G_{90} \le 0$ apart.}}
{\fig{fig:index_limits}.}

\issue{it:tpdays}{\prioA\ All \code{tp_*}: coverage and freshness}
{\pts{\item \textbf{Start:} 6 July 2026, with about 5\,000 rows a day.
\item \textbf{Changes:} on 1 August it goes to about 300\,000 rows; on 19 August the session changes.
\item \textbf{Frozen days:} 9 days are exactly the same as the day before (21--24 and 26 August; 4, 11, 14 and 15 October), in every brand.
\item \textbf{Affects:} all \code{tp_*}.}}
{}
{\pts{\item Do not use the \code{tp_*} of the table in the history; use the recomputed ones.
\item A freshness check in the daily process.}}
{\fig{fig:tp_daily}, \fig{fig:night_days}.}

\end{enumerate}

\section{Medium Priority}
Wrong in many rows but not used for decisions, or already fixed from now on. The \code{tp_*} columns exist since 6 July 2026.
\begin{enumerate}[resume*=issues]

\issue{it:session}{\prioM\ \code{tp_avg_session_duration_secs}, \code{tp_median_session_duration_secs}}
{\pts{\item \textbf{Sessions that never close:} 0.6\% last more than 1 day, up to 28 days.
\item \textbf{Sessions of one event:} 1.8\% last exactly 1~s.
\item \textbf{Outside 10~s to 4~h:} 8.4\% (mean) and 6.6\% (median); 14.1\% in brand 73.
\item \textbf{Other facts:} the window is 90 days, and the value is not \code{play_secs / sessions} (it matches in 2.9\%).
\item \textbf{Part are sports bets:} the players who bet on sports (providers 58 and 50) have 7 times more sessions over 1 day (0.57\% against 0.08\%) and hold 64\% of the cases; the other 36\% is casino.}}
{\pts{\item \textbf{Table (deduced):} $\bar d = \operatorname{mean}_{s} (b_s - a_s)$ over 90 days, open sessions not closed.
\item \textbf{Correct:} session = run of bets with no gap over 30~min; $\bar d = \operatorname{mean}\{t_{\text{last}} - t_{\text{first}} : n_s \ge 2\}$; one-bet sessions counted apart.}}
{\pts{\item Rebuild the sessions from each bet: only 0.3\% stay outside the range (mean per player) and the median hardly changes (25 vs 28~min).
\item Note: even then there are sessions of up to 30~h of non-stop play, probably automatic.
\item Separate the sports bets (providers 58 and 50) before building the sessions: a sports bet stays open until the match ends.}}
{\fig{fig:session_avg}, \fig{fig:session_median}, \fig{fig:session_brands}, \fig{fig:session_checks}, \fig{fig:session_days}, \fig{fig:sessions_products}.}

\issue{it:gap}{\prioM\ \code{tp_avg_session_gap_hours}}
{\pts{\item \textbf{Negative gaps:} 0.91\% of the rows, down to $-262$~h; a session starts before the previous one ends.
\item \textbf{Where:} every day and every brand, more in 72 and 73.
\item \textbf{Not sports:} 60\% of the negative gaps are of players who only play casino (0.70\% of their rows).}}
{\pts{\item \textbf{Definition:} $g_s = a_{s+1} - b_s$.
\item \textbf{Correct:} with the 30-minute cut, always $g_s > 30$~min.}}
{\pts{\item Calculate the gap with the rebuilt sessions.}}
{\fig{fig:session_checks}, \fig{fig:sessions_products}.}

\issue{it:playsecs}{\prioM\ \code{play_secs_today}, \code{play_secs_l7d}, \code{play_secs_l30d}, \code{play_secs_l90d}, \code{play_secs_l180d}}
{\pts{\item \textbf{More time than the day has:} 3.7\% of the days with play go over 24~h (6.0\% in brand 73).
\item \textbf{Maximum:} 26 million seconds (305 days) in September; 210 million in the history.
\item \textbf{Affects:} \code{engagement_score} and \code{session_intensity_score}.
\item \textbf{Mostly sports:} the days with sports bets go over 24~h in 13.9\% to 14.6\% of the rows, against 1.05\% of the casino-only days, and hold 77\% of the cases. In casino, the error of adding up games played at the same time is left.}}
{\pts{\item \textbf{Table (hypothesis):} sum per game, $\sum_j \text{secs}_{j,d}$, so games played at the same time count twice.
\item \textbf{Correct:} the union of the session intervals of the day, $\bigl|\bigcup_s [a_s, b_s] \cap d\bigr| \le 86\,400$~s.}}
{\pts{\item Calculate it with the rebuilt sessions of each day.
\item Do not compute the time of play of sports bets: in sports it measures how long the bet is open.}}
{\fig{fig:session_checks}, \fig{fig:sessions_products}.}

\issue{it:night}{\prioM\ \code{tp_night_index}}
{\pts{\item \textbf{Not a share:} it reaches 4, with values on $4/k$ (4, 2, 4/3, 1\ldots).
\item \textbf{How much:} 8.1\% of the rows are above 1.
\item \textbf{Also:} the night hours or the time zone seem different (median 0.20 vs 0.41).}}
{\pts{\item \textbf{Table:} $\dfrac{S_{\text{night}}/S}{6/24}$, an index against an even spread.
\item \textbf{Correct (M127):} $S_{\text{night}}/S \in [0,1]$, from 00:00 to 06:00 local time.}}
{\pts{\item Calculate it as a share, from each bet.}}
{\fig{fig:tp_scale}, \fig{fig:tp_above}, \fig{fig:night}, \fig{fig:night_days}.}

\issue{it:rgnight}{\prioM\ \code{rg_night_play_ratio}}
{\pts{\item \textbf{It should be equal to} \code{tp_night_index}, but it only matches in 91.5\% of the rows.
\item \textbf{It looks like} a version capped at 1.
\item \textbf{Affects:} \code{risk_score}.}}
{\pts{\item \textbf{Workbook:} $S_{\text{night}}/S$.
\item \textbf{Table:} always in $[0,1]$, but different from \code{tp_night_index}.}}
{\pts{\item Replace it with the recomputed night share (point~\ref{it:night}).}}
{Annex~\ref{sec:columns}: it matches \code{tp_night_index} in 91.5\% of the rows.}

\issue{it:weekend}{\prioM\ \code{tp_weekend_index}}
{\pts{\item \textbf{Not a share:} it reaches 3.5, with values on $3.5/k$.
\item \textbf{How much:} 23.4\% of the rows are above 1.}}
{\pts{\item \textbf{Table:} $\dfrac{S_{\text{weekend}}/S}{2/7}$.
\item \textbf{Correct (M147):} $S_{\text{Saturday and Sunday}}/S \in [0,1]$, local time.}}
{\pts{\item Calculate it as a share. Multiplying the table by 2/7 already gives almost the same (median 0.40 vs 0.41).}}
{\fig{fig:tp_scale}, \fig{fig:tp_above}, \fig{fig:weekend}.}

\issue{it:entropy}{\prioM\ \code{tp_hourly_entropy}}
{\pts{\item \textbf{Wrong scale:} it is in bits and not normalised; it reaches 4.56.
\item \textbf{How much:} 27.8\% of the rows are above 1 (the workbook says 0--1).
\item \textbf{Evidence of the base:} exact masses at $\log_2 2 = 1$, $\log_2 3 = 1.585$, $\log_2 4 = 2$, and none at $\ln 2$ or $\ln 3$.}}
{\pts{\item \textbf{Table:} $H = -\sum_h p_h \log_2 p_h \in [0, 4.58]$.
\item \textbf{Correct:} $H / \log_2 24 \in [0,1]$, with $p_h = S_{\text{hour } h}/S$ in local time.}}
{\pts{\item Divide by $\log_2 24$, or calculate it from each bet (median 0.53 vs 0.47).}}
{\fig{fig:tp_scale}, \fig{fig:tp_above}, \fig{fig:entropy}.}

\issue{it:depositrecency}{\prioM\ \code{deposit_recency_score}}
{\pts{\item \textbf{It measures the wrong thing:} it is a deposit score, but it uses the days since the \emph{last bet} (\code{days_since_bet}), not since the \emph{last deposit}.
\item \textbf{Example:} a player who bets every day but has not deposited for two months looks as if they deposited yesterday.
\item \textbf{The error is in the workbook definition,} not in the table: the table follows that formula.
\item \textbf{It also has the leak} of \code{days_since_bet} (point~\ref{it:recency}).}}
{\pts{\item \textbf{Workbook (M113) and table:} 100 if there is a deposit today; otherwise $100\,(1 - \min(1, \text{days\_since\_bet}/30))$.

\section{Impossible and Suspicious Values, by Brand and Day}\label{sec:flags}

\textbf{Why it matters.} An \emph{impossible} value (a share above 1, a session of more than a day) cannot come from real data: it is direct evidence that something is calculated wrong. A \emph{suspicious} value is possible but very rare (more than 50 deposits in a day) and needs a check. Looking at them by brand and day shows where the problem comes from:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{In every brand and every day} $\Rightarrow$ it is a calculation error.
  \item \textbf{Only in one brand or on some days} $\Rightarrow$ it is a specific problem (some data, a release, some players) or real behaviour to check.
\end{itemize}

Each column is on a grey or white band, with its name, its status and, if it is in the list, the priority and number of its point, once. \emph{Rule}: the one that runs, DuckDB SQL on the columns of the table. \emph{Pass}: \% of the rows of the two snapshots that meet the rule (criterion 3), empty if it is not calculated here; green if it passes ($\ge 99\%$), orange if almost ($95\%$ to $99\%$) and red if it fails. A wrong column can pass its checks at 100\%: the error is in its definition or in an input, and it shows when it is compared with the daily tables.
